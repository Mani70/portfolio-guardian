# Audience Psychology of Short-Form Finance Video (Reels/Shorts/TikTok), with an Indian Hindi/Hinglish Lens

Evidence labels: **[PR]** peer-reviewed · **[PD]** platform statement/data · **[LS]** large survey or regulator dataset · **[VB]** vendor/practitioner blog · **[AN]** anecdote/inference. Method note: research ran on WebSearch result summaries. A direct WebFetch of sebi.gov.in failed (DNS blocked), so SEBI figures come from press coverage of SEBI releases. Classic pre-2018 papers are marked *(classic)*.

## 1. Stop scrolling and keep watching: curiosity gaps, open loops, surprise, narrative

### Takeaway
The theory is strong: curiosity is the felt gap between what someone knows and what they want to know, and narrative "transportation" holds attention and lowers counter-arguing. Direct peer-reviewed tests on short video are thin. Instagram itself names watch time as a top ranking signal, so a hook that opens a specific, answerable gap in the first 1-3 seconds and pays it off is the best-supported design.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR, classic 1994]** Loewenstein's information-gap theory says curiosity is a deprivation that arises when people notice a specific gap in their knowledge. Curiosity rises when people already know a little, so they can see the gap — [Loewenstein 1994, Psych Bulletin](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75); summarized as "a cognitive induced deprivation that arises from the perception of a gap in knowledge" — [Frontiers in Medicine 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.772956/pdf)
- **[PR]** The relationship is an inverted U: gaps that are too large can produce helplessness instead of curiosity — [ABSEL paper applying Loewenstein](https://absel-ojs-ttu.tdl.org/absel/article/view/751/720)
- **[PR]** In two online trivia experiments, more available candidate answers predicted higher curiosity, moderate confidence and better memory five days later. Curiosity at moderate confidence improves retention — [Oxford Experimental Psychology publication page](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/publication/2440158)
- **[PR, classic 2000]** Narrative transportation (absorption in a story) increases story-consistent beliefs and reduces critical counter-arguing — [Green & Brock 2000, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701)
- **[PD]** Mosseri (Jan 2025) told creators to track average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach, and listed watch time first — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-reveals-key-factors-for-boosting-reach-8646916)
- **[VB]** "Open loop / cliffhanger" retention claims for YouTube come from practitioner blogs, not data — [Autonolab blog](https://blog.autonolab.com/blog/2025-08-22-open-loops-and-curiosity-gaps-the-psychology-of-binge-watching/)

### Inferences
- **[AN]** Hook pattern: start from a fact the viewer half-knows, then name the gap. For example: "Nifty 1% gira — par aapke SIP ka kya hua? Answer 20 second mein." Avoid gaps the viewer cannot place at all (inverted-U), such as "Vega crush explained".
- **[AN]** Always close the loop you open. An unresolved or clickbait gap feels like a broken promise, and that hurts the trust that finance content depends on.
- **[AN]** A micro-story (a person with a problem, a choice, then the outcome) uses narrative transportation, and it also lowers resistance. That is a reason to stay ethical about which conclusions you embed.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study testing curiosity-gap hooks on Reels/Shorts retention specifically.
- The Zeigarnik effect (better memory for unfinished tasks) is widely cited by marketers. Its replication record is mixed, and I could not verify a recent meta-analysis in this session, so don't treat "open loops" as a proven memory effect.
- No source here tested surprise or expectancy violation in short video. That mechanism stays theory-based.

## 2. Faces and people on screen and in thumbnails

### Takeaway
Faces reliably correlate with more engagement on still images (1.1M-photo Instagram study). Evidence that faces lift CTR on YouTube thumbnails or Reels covers comes mostly from vendors. Number, age and gender of faces mattered little, which suggests relatable stock or illustrated people (a worried investor, a family) may also work. This is untested for video.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR, classic 2014, correlational]** In 1.1M Instagram photos, those with faces were 38% more likely to get likes and 32% more likely to get comments. The number of faces, their age and their gender made no meaningful difference. Posting too often reduced likes about twice as fast as it reduced comments. The authors did not establish why — [Georgia Tech news](https://www.news.gatech.edu/news/2014/03/20/face-it-instagram-pictures-faces-are-more-popular); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/03/140320134724.htm)
- **[VB]** A Kapwing/NeoMam AI analysis of big-channel thumbnails found surprise and happiness each in about 27% of faces. This measured how often creators use each expression, not how well it performs — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/which-emotion-works-best-for-youtube-thumbnails/)
- **[VB, unverified]** A claimed "25-40% CTR lift" for emotive faces came from a vendor that sells face tools and cited no study — [Lovart blog](https://www.lovart.ai/de/blog/youtube-thumbnail-design-science-2027); see also [ThumbnailTest guide](https://thumbnailtest.com/guides/face-in-youtube-thumbnail/)
- **[PR, classic 2014]** Emotional contagion travels through feeds even without face-to-face contact: reducing positive posts in users' feeds led those users to write fewer positive posts — [Kramer et al. 2014, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1320040111)

### Inferences
- **[AN]** For a faceless or animated channel, a human figure or illustrated character with a readable emotion (a worried uncle at the shop counter, a young couple looking at an EMI bill) is a reasonable bet, because the Instagram data showed the effect did not depend on who the face was. Test it rather than assume it.
- **[AN]** Match the expression to the payoff. A shocked face on a calm SIP explainer is a mismatch, and it costs trust.
- **[AN]** A recurring creator face or mascot also builds the parasocial and recognition effects covered in section 4, which stock faces cannot.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or platform data compared the creator's own face with relatable stock faces for short video, or tested faces in Reels cover frames.

## 3. Sharing and sends

### Takeaway
People share content that is high-arousal (awe, anger, anxiety), useful, and makes them look good. Instagram says sends per reach matter most for reaching non-followers. For finance, the shareable emotions are awe ("this compounding math is crazy") and useful anxiety ("check this before you sign"), both packaged as help for a specific friend or family member.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR, classic 2012]** Across about 3 months of NYT articles, positive content was more viral than negative. Within that, high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) increased sharing and low-arousal sadness decreased it — [Berger & Milkman 2012, JMR (postprint)](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf); [Social Science Space](https://www.socialsciencespace.com/2023/05/a-viral-paper-on-determining-what-makes-online-content-viral/)
- **[PR/book]** Berger's STEPPS framework names six drivers: Social currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical value and Stories — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-secret-to-online-success-what-makes-content-go-viral)
- **[PD]** Mosseri: sends count slightly more than likes for reaching non-followers, and likes count more with existing followers. He also said sends per reach "correlate more... with overall reach than anything else" in his experience — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034/); [t3n](https://t3n.de/news/?p=1671110)
- **[LS]** About 59% of Indian investors rely on friends, family or colleagues for market information. Sharing within the family is therefore a core channel — [Outlook Money on SEBI Survey 2025](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/equity/sebi-investor-survey-2025-new-investors-listen-to-friends-feeds-and-finfluencers)

### Inferences
- **[AN]** Design for the send. Name who should receive the Reel: "Ye papa ko bhejo jo FD ke alawa kuch nahi maante", or "Us dost ko bhejo jo F&O mein paisa daal raha hai". This uses practical value plus social currency (the sender looks savvy and caring).
- **[AN]** Prefer awe and empowerment, and use anxiety only when you also give an action. Anger and fear can get sends but erode long-term trust (see section 6).
- **[AN]** Triggers: tie content to recurring cues (the 3:30 pm close, budget day, results season, salary day on the 1st, Diwali Muhurat trading) so viewers are reminded of the content and share it.

### Gaps
- No study isolated which emotions drive sharing of finance content specifically, or of content in Indian languages.

## 4. Why people follow and come back: future value, series, identity, parasocial trust, habit

### Takeaway
A follow is a bet on future value. Consistent series formats make that value predictable, parasocial bonds turn viewers into loyal followers, and expertise and trustworthiness (not attractiveness) build those bonds. Return viewing rests on daily cues and rituals. Peer-reviewed evidence on streaks and variable reward in finance video is missing.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR, classic 1956]** Parasocial interaction is the one-sided "intimacy at a distance" audiences form with media figures — [Horton & Wohl 1956, Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1080/00332747.1956.11023049)
- **[PR, 2026, n=407, survey]** Perceived expertise and trustworthiness predicted parasocial relationships with influencers. Attractiveness did not. Parasocial relationship predicted purchase intention. (Sample: Indonesian beauty-influencer followers) — [Frontiers in Communication 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2026.1723759/full)
- **[PR, review]** A literature review describes finfluencers as key intermediaries for young investors. It flags misinformation, undisclosed sponsorship and pump-and-dump risks, and says parasocial effects in finance are under-studied — [GJEIS finfluencer review](https://gjeis.com/index.php/gjeis/article/view/25)
- **[LS]** In a 2024 CFA Institute survey of 1,615 Indian investors, 82% of those influenced by social media acted on the advice they saw. Only 2% of the 51 finfluencers reviewed were SEBI-registered — [CFA Institute "Clicks and Credibility"](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/surveys/2025/clicks-and-credibility); [PDF](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/docs/surveys/clicks-and-credibility.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[AN]** Make the follow reason explicit and repeatable: "Roz 7 baje: aaj market mein kya hua, 60 second mein." A named, numbered series ("Day 14 of 100 Money Lessons") turns a single view into an expectation of future value and also creates an open loop across episodes.
- **[AN]** Appointment viewing fits markets well, because the NSE closes at 3:30 pm IST and gives a natural daily trigger for an evening recap. A consistent time slot and opening line ("Namaste, market band ho chuka hai...") build the ritual.
- **[AN]** Identity: speak to one persona ("salaried 25-35, tier-2, pehli baar invest kar raha") so viewers think "ye mere jaisa hai". Expertise cues (data, charts, SEBI sources) build the parasocial bond more than polish does.
- **[AN]** Variable reward comes naturally: the market differs every day (a surprise mover, a "myth of the day"). Streaks only make sense if the platform supports them. Neither Reels nor Shorts expose viewer streaks.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evidence on follow conversion from series formats, or on streaks and variable reward in short-form finance. I found no finance-specific parasocial study with investing outcomes.

## 5. Finance-specific psychology: loss aversion, FOMO, scam fear, shame, trust

### Takeaway
Indian audiences are strongly loss-averse (about 80% prefer capital preservation), wary because of complexity and trust deficits, and still exposed to get-rich-quick pulls (more than 90% of F&O traders lose, yet most keep trading). Ethical content leads with loss avoidance and clarity and shows its sources. Fear-mongering works short-term but fights the trust that drives follows.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR, classic 1979]** Losses loom larger than equivalent gains (prospect theory) — [Kahneman & Tversky 1979, Econometrica](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185)
- **[LS]** About 80% of Indian households, and 79% of Gen-Z respondents, prefer capital preservation over higher returns. The main barriers are complexity, lack of knowledge, trust deficits and fear of losses. 22% of non-investors intend to invest within a year — [SEBI press release, Sep 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2025/sebi-releases-investor-survey-2025-findings-to-guide-inclusive-and-responsible-investing_96984.html) via [TeamLease RegTech](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/47345/sebi-investor-survey-2025-findings-to-guide-inclusive-and-responsible-); [Angel One](https://oga-prod.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-report-reveals-low-household-participation-in-securities-at-9-5)
- **[LS]** In FY22-24, 93% of about 1.13 crore individual F&O traders lost money, with net losses of about ₹1.81 lakh crore. More than 75% of loss-makers kept trading — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/93-percentage-of-individual-traders-lost-rs18-lakh-crore-in-equity-fo-in-past-3-years-sebi/75210.html); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/93-of-individual-traders-suffered-losses-in-fo-in-last-3-years-sebi-study)
- **[LS]** In FY25, net individual F&O losses rose about 41% to about ₹1.06 lakh crore, and more than 91% of traders lost. Unique traders fell from 61.4 lakh (Q1) to 42.7 lakh (Q4) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html). Loss rates rose with experience, from about 91% in year one to more than 95% after four or more years, and only 0.5% were profitable every year — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/only-9-of-options-traders-make-money-will-you-4063147); [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266)
- **[AN/journalism]** The pull of "quick money in minutes" keeps drawing people in — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/only-9-of-options-traders-make-money-will-you-4063147)
- **[LS]** 62% of retail investors say finfluencers have influenced their choices, and 93% rate them moderately to highly credible. These figures come from secondary coverage and are not confirmed against SEBI's own text — [Moneylife MAS360](https://mas360.moneylife.in/article/sebi-survey-reveals-indian-investors-prefer-social-media-influencers-over-traditional-financial-advisors/4832.html)

### Inferences
- **[AN]** Loss-framed hooks fit the audience. "Ye 3 galtiyan aapka SIP return aadha kar deti hain" uses loss aversion ethically, because it helps the viewer avoid a real loss. "Market crash aane wala hai!!" with no data is fear-mongering.
- **[AN]** Counter the "quick money" pull with vivid, sourced reality: "SEBI data: 100 mein se 91 F&O traders ne paisa gawaya". This creates awe and anxiety, is shareable, and builds trust.
- **[AN]** Remove shame: "Agar aapko XIRR nahi pata, aap akele nahi ho". Saying so normalizes not knowing, which reduces the trust and knowledge barriers SEBI identified.
- **[AN]** Trust signals: on-screen source cards ("Source: SEBI study, Jul 2025"), a "not investment advice / educational" disclaimer, no stock tips or targets, no guaranteed returns, and disclosed conflicts. Regulators are tightening rules on unregistered finfluencers, so staying educational is both safer and more credible.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous study comparing fear-framed and empowerment-framed finance video on follows or retention.

## 6. Indian retail-investor audience: who, where they learn, language, culture

### Takeaway
The growth audience is young, outside the metros and learns socially. About 60% saw market content on social media in the previous 3 months, and Indic-language viewing preference is higher outside metros. Hinglish is the natural register, but I found no survey that measures it for finance specifically.

### Cited Findings
- **[LS]** SEBI Investor Survey 2025: 63% of households (about 21.3 crore) know at least one securities product, but only 9.5% (about 3.21 crore) invest, about 15% in urban and 6% in rural areas. The survey also found strong demand for regional-language financial education and simpler digital platforms — [SEBI press release](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2025/sebi-releases-investor-survey-2025-findings-to-guide-inclusive-and-responsible-investing_96984.html) via [Angel One](https://oga-prod.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-report-reveals-low-household-participation-in-securities-at-9-5)
- **[LS]** About 60% recalled seeing securities-market content on social media in the previous 3 months. About 59% of investors rely on friends, family or colleagues, and about 56% on finfluencers on YouTube and Instagram — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/equity/sebi-investor-survey-2025-new-investors-listen-to-friends-feeds-and-finfluencers)
- **[PD/exchange]** In January 2025, the median age of new NSE investors was about 32, with about 40% under 30. States outside the top 10 hold 27% of the investor base, and investors are spread across 99.85% of pin codes — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/nse-registered-investor-base-growth-tops-130-million-10-million-added-in-7-months-as-retail-participation-deepens); [NSE PR PDF](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/2025-01/PR_cc_22012025_0.pdf). One outlet puts under-30s at 56.2% of FY26 new registrations. This likely uses a different base, so treat it as not comparable — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/equity/new-investor-registrations-on-nse-at-six-month-high-in-july-know-which-states-led-the-surge)
- **[LS]** Among F&O traders, the under-30 share rose from 31% to 43% (FY23 to FY24), and more than 72% were from beyond the top 30 cities, compared with 62% for mutual fund investors — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/amp/story/invest/93-of-individual-traders-suffered-losses-in-fo-in-last-3-years-sebi-study). FY26: under-30s are 43% of traders and 88.5% of them lost money. B30 cities account for 67% of traders and 58% of losses — [Morung Express/IANS](https://morungexpress.com/885-pc-of-traders-under-30-lost-money-in-fo-trading-in-fy26-sebi-study)
- **[PD/LS, dated]** 93% of YouTube viewers in India watch Indic-language content (2019 Google/Kantar figure). Indic preference is 53% in non-metros versus 39% in metros, and Hindi leads among regional languages — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/93-youtube-viewers-watch-indian-language-content-mindshare-e4m-content-marketing-report-134060.html); [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/youtube-india-claims-60-of-its-users-come-from-smaller-towns)
- **[VB]** The claim that Hinglish is the "natural register" for social media comes from a marketing agency and has no data behind it — [JustWords](https://www.justwords.in/blog/regional-language-content-marketing-india/)

### Inferences
- **[AN]** Core persona: a 22-35-year-old in a tier-2/3 city in the Hindi belt, new to markets or tempted by F&O, who learns from family and Reels. Speak Hinglish, with Hindi syntax and English finance terms such as SIP, EMI and stop-loss, and explain jargon on the first use.
- **[AN]** Cultural hooks give instant schema: cricket (a Test match vs T20 for SIP vs intraday), shaadi budget, EMI traps, chai-tapri tips ("tapri wale bhaiya ka tip"), Bollywood lines, and jugaad framed as a smart-but-safe hack. Each lowers cognitive load by mapping onto something already known.
- **[AN]** Because family is a top information source, "ghar walon ke liye" framing raises sends.

### Gaps
- No survey quantifies Hinglish vs pure Hindi preference for finance content. I found no 2025 Market Pulse split of demat accounts by city tier. SEBI's original survey PDF was not accessible (fetch blocked).

## 7. Comments: why people comment and whether replies matter

### Takeaway
Observational and quasi-experimental studies find that creator replies and hearts go with more engagement, especially in small comment sections. Easy, low-effort prompts most plausibly drive comments, but that rests on practitioner consensus rather than controlled studies.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR, observational]** In 87,232 comments on 647 beauty-creator videos, more creator replies were associated with higher video engagement. The effect was stronger when fewer people were commenting — [Online Information Review 2023](https://www.emerald.com/oir/article/47/6/1083/318055/The-effect-of-YouTube-comment-interaction-on-video)
- **[PR, quasi-experimental]** A creator "heart" soon after posting raised the visibility of that comment and nudged other viewers to comment, increasing engagement over time — [arXiv 2404.03612](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03612v1)
- **[PR, 2014]** Faces were linked to 32% more comments on Instagram photos (see section 2) — [Georgia Tech](https://www.news.gatech.edu/news/2014/03/20/face-it-instagram-pictures-faces-are-more-popular)

### Inferences
- **[AN]** Use one-word or one-number prompts, such as "Comment karo: SIP ya FD?" or "Aapki pehli investment kis age mein thi?", or a "comment 'PDF' for..." prompt. Questions about the viewer's own experience are low-effort and help build identity.
- **[AN]** Reply and heart in the first hour, while the comment section is small, because that is where the observed effect was strongest. Turn good questions into the next Reel ("Rahul ne poocha..."). This rewards the commenter, creates a reason to return and signals community.
- **[AN]** Avoid controversy bait on money topics. It brings comments but attracts scam bots and "tips" spam, which damages trust.

### Gaps
- No randomized study links creator replies to return visits, and none covers finance or Indian audiences.

## 8. Cognitive load: information density, one idea, repetition, length

### Takeaway
Shorter videos and energetic delivery go with more engagement (MOOC data). For finance explainers that implies one idea per Reel, numbers that are shown as well as spoken, and a closing recap. I found no peer-reviewed short-video data on how much information viewers absorb per second.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR, classic 2014, MOOC observational]** Video length was "by far the most significant indicator of engagement", and engagement peaked at under 6 minutes. Fast, enthusiastic speakers held viewers better (a reported figure for the most engaging speakers is about 254 wpm, with 185-254 wpm suggested in later summaries). The study measured engagement, not comprehension — [Guo, Kim & Rubin via UBC](https://flexible.learning.ubc.ca/?p=2444); [Delta State summary](https://www.deltastate.edu/fordctl/wp-content/uploads/sites/71/2019/09/Viewer-Retention-in-Videos.pdf)
- **[VB]** eMarketer argues Gen-Z finance content should accommodate multitasking viewers, for example with on-screen text plus audio — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/key-creating-financial-content-that-resonates-with-gen-z-let-them-multitask)

### Inferences
- **[AN]** Use one claim, one number and one action per Reel. Put each key number on screen as text while you say it (dual coding), and repeat the core takeaway at the end ("Yaad rakho: ...").
- **[AN]** Fast Hinglish delivery helps retention, but finance numbers need a beat to land. Pause about 0.5 s on each key figure and round the numbers ("₹1 lakh crore" rather than "₹1,05,603 crore").
- **[AN]** Length: no platform data here fixes an ideal Reel length. Choose the shortest length that fully closes the loop, and use retention graphs to cut dead seconds.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study gives absorption limits per second for short vertical video, or Hindi/Hinglish speech-rate comprehension norms. Mayer's segmenting and signalling principles are relevant but were not verified in this session.
