## 1. Options for showing people: evidence, legal risk and engagement upside

### Takeaway
"Make it look real" by using pictures of people who are not real (AI faces) or who never agreed to the message (stock models shown as victims or investors) is the highest-risk route under 2026 Indian rules and platform rules. In a market already full of deepfake stock-tip scams, it also works against trust. Illustrated characters or a clearly fictional mascot carry the least risk. Neutral, labelled stock photos are acceptable within licence limits. Photoreal AI people and AI talking-head avatars should be avoided.

### Cited Findings

**(1) Licensed stock photos (Pexels / Unsplash / Pixabay)**
- Pexels' licence bars showing identifiable people "in a bad light or in a way that is offensive" and bars implying that people or brands in an image endorse you. The Pexels help centre gives examples: depicting someone in criminal activity, with a medical ailment, or in a pornographic context. **[platform policy]** — [Pexels help: rules for using photos](https://help.pexels.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042332714-What-are-the-rules-for-using-Pexels-photos-or-videos); [Pexels licence (archived)](https://web-archive.nli.org.il/National_Library/mp_/https://pexels.com/license/)
- Unsplash: "if there's an identifiable person in the image, you may need his or her permission before you can use the image in certain ways". The user is "solely responsible" for not infringing third-party rights. Third-party guides add that commercial use of identifiable people may need model releases. **[platform policy]** — [Unsplash help: identifiable person](https://help.unsplash.com/en/articles/2646379-what-if-there-s-a-brand-or-identifiable-person-depicted-in-a-photo-that-i-download); [Pixsy Unsplash guide](https://pixsy.com/image-licensing/unsplash-guide) **[vendor]**
- Implication for finance (inference from the licence wording above): showing a stock model as a scam victim, a person in debt, a gambler losing money at F&O, or a person "who made 10x" falls inside "bad light" and/or "implied endorsement". Neutral roles such as "a salaried professional checking an app" are fine.

**(2) AI-generated photorealistic people**
- India's IT Rules amendment (G.S.R. 120(E)) was notified on 10 Feb 2026 and took effect on 20 Feb 2026. It brings "synthetically generated information" (SGI) into the rules: content that "appears real or indistinguishable from a natural person or real-world event". Routine good-faith editing is excluded. Platforms must apply a prominent label and permanent provenance metadata, and must proactively detect and label synthetic audio-visual content. **[regulator/law, via law-firm summaries]** — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/story/govt-tightens-rules-on-ai-generated-content-mandates-labels-and-faster-takedowns-515505-2026-02-10) **[journalism]**; [Lexplosion](https://lexplosion.in/meity-notifies-information-technology-intermediary-guidelines-and-digital-media-ethics-code-amendment-rules-2026-introducing-compliance-framework-for-synthetically-generated-information-and-revisin/)
- Meta (Feb 2024) said it would label AI images using C2PA/IPTC metadata. It requires users to self-disclose photorealistic video and realistic-sounding audio, and may penalise those who don't. From July 2024 the label became "AI info". Meta admitted it "can't yet detect" such signals in other companies' video and audio. **[platform policy, via journalism]** — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2024/02/06/meta-will-label-ai-generated-images-across-facebook-instagram-threads/); [Thurrott](https://www.thurrott.com/a-i/297436/meta-will-soon-label-ai-generated-images-posted-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads); [MakeInfluence C2PA explainer](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/content-credentials-c2pa-how-platforms-label-ai-assisted-content) **[vendor]**
- Detection is error-prone: real photos have been labelled AI, and some AI images carry no label. **[journalism]** — [heise](https://heise.de/-9776887)
- No source showed that the "AI info" label itself cuts Reels distribution (see Gaps).

**(3) Illustrated characters, mascots, virtual influencers**
- Under ASCI's influencer guidelines, virtual influencers (computer-generated "people" or avatars) must disclose up front, "as a primary focus", that consumers are not dealing with a real human. The obligation falls on whoever controls the posts. **[self-regulatory code, via law-firm]** — [Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/media-hotline/social-media-vs-reality-check-ascis-new-influencer-guidelines-4713); [VirtualHumans.org](https://virtualhumans.org/article/official-india-moves-to-regulate-virtual-influencers) **[journalism]**
- A May 2026 law-firm note says ASCI's updated guidelines explicitly cover AI-generated virtual influencers. Every promotional post must carry both an AI disclosure and a paid-content disclosure. Single source, not checked against ASCI's text. ASCI codes are not statutory, but the CCPA increasingly treats non-disclosure as an unfair trade practice. **[law-firm]** — [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/asci-influencer-advertising-guidelines-2026-disclosure-rules-for-paid-content-and-ai-influencers)
- Kyra, "India's first virtual influencer" (launched Jan 2022), reached about 100K followers within months, about 200K+ later and about 242K on Instagram by Aug 2026 (aggregator figure). Brand deals were boAt and Titan Eye, with a reported ₹5-10 lakh per video. Early reactions included "Black Mirror" comparisons and people calling it fake. Indian virtual influencers have not reached mainstream commercial success. **[journalism / aggregator]** — [VirtualHumans](https://virtualhumans.org/article/kyra-indias-first-virtual-influencer-has-over-100-000-followers); [SheThePeople](https://www.shethepeople.tv/news/indias-first-virtual-influencer-kyra); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/experts-speak/is-india-lagging-in-virtual-influencer-race-8887887); [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/kyraonig/)
- Research on virtual vs human influencer trust conflicts:
  - Böhndel et al. 2023 found no significant credibility or competence difference, only likeability. **[peer-reviewed, small journal]** — [SSOAR](https://ssoar.info/ssoar/handle/document/90190)
  - Hofeditz et al. (ECIS 2022, n=112) found humans rated higher on trust, social presence and humanness. **[peer-reviewed conference]** — [AIS eLibrary](https://aisel.aisnet.org/ecis2022_rip/27)
  - Lee et al. (n=130) found virtual influencers perceived as *more* authentic, with authenticity leading to trust and then purchase intent. **[peer-reviewed, venue unconfirmed]** — [HSTalks](https://hstalks.com/article/7571/ai-driven-influencer-marketing-comparing-the-effec/)
- A 2025 International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction study (virtual influencers) found that parasocial relationships positively influence trust and word-of-mouth. **[peer-reviewed]** — via [KNU research portal](https://research.knu.ac.kr/en/publications/the-moderating-role-of-homophily-and-need-for-uniqueness-in-the-r/)
- Mascots:
  - Duolingo-specific evidence is weak. A 2026 conference paper (n=164) found perceived brand anthropomorphism predicts continued-use intention. **[conference, preliminary]** — [Atlantis Press PRMC 2026](https://www.atlantis-press.com/proceedings/prmc-26/126026899)
  - A 2024 thesis found mascot anthropomorphism did not by itself significantly change brand trust. **[thesis]** — [NCCU](https://thesis.lib.nccu.edu.tw/detail/80269998dbf1f172d768dd6ffa87a0ab/)

**(4) Real public figures (Harshad Mehta, founders, CEOs) in news/history stories**
- Delhi HC personality-rights orders in Sept 2025 covered Aishwarya Rai Bachchan (ex parte interim injunction, Justice Tejas Karia), Abhishek Bachchan and Karan Johar. The orders protect name, image, voice and likeness against unauthorised commercial use, AI/deepfakes, morphing and merchandise. The court noted that misuse affects the "right to live with dignity". Earlier orders covered Anil Kapoor and Jackie Shroff. **[journalism / legal commentary]** — [OECD AI incident](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents/2025-09-15-91a5); [BananaIP](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/?p=145560); [Bar & Bench topic page](https://www.barandbench.com/topic/personality-rights)
- Copyright: Section 52(1)(a) fair dealing covers criticism/review and "reporting current events". The use must fit an enumerated purpose (narrower than US fair use). There is no safe-harbour amount, and courts weigh purpose, nature, amount and market effect. In ANI v. YouTubers, a blanket takedown was refused and surgical removal ordered. YouTube's automated systems may still flag fair-dealing uses. **[law-firm / legal commentary]** — [Cyril Amarchand blog (2025)](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2025/06/fair-dealing-in-the-digital-age-navigating-copyright-for-news-and-online-content-in-india/); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/columns/youtubers-in-peril-why-indias-fair-use-laws-need-urgent-clarification); [SpicyIP on Aaj Tak v Newslaundry](https://spicyip.com/2021/11/aaj-tak-v-newslaundry-assessing-the-fair-dealing-question.html)
- Finance-specific deepfake context: NSE warned twice in 2024 (10 Apr and 10 Jun) about deepfake videos of MD & CEO Ashishkumar Chauhan "recommending stocks". The Bombay HC ordered removal, and MeitY urged fast takedowns. **[regulator/exchange + journalism]** — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/beware-of-deepfake-of-ceo-recommending-stocks-says-national-stock-exchange-124041000420_1.html); [NSE PR June 2024](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/2024-07/PR_cc_10062024.pdf); [Business Standard explainer](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/deepfake-videos-of-the-nse-chief-misleading-investors-what-you-must-know-124061100306_1.html)

**(5) AI talking-head avatars (HeyGen/Synthesia style)**
- Baake, Schmitt & Metag (2025, JCOM, n≈491; science communication) found more realistic avatars rated *more* trustworthy, with no uncanny-valley drop. Male avatars were rated more competent than female ones. **[peer-reviewed]** — [JCOM](https://jcom.sissa.it/article/pubid/JCOM_2402_2025_A03/); [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/ai-avatar-communication-28649/)
- A 2021 experiment (n=228) found uncanny-valley perceptions mediated lower affect-based trust in some talking-head setups. **[peer-reviewed]** — [Cyberpsychology, Behavior & Social Networking](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/cyber.2020.0175)
- A thesis found avatars suitable for factual formats but with perceptual limits compared with real presenters. **[thesis]** — [hebis](https://hlbrm.pur.hebis.de/xmlui/handle/123456789/452)
- A systematic review of uncanny-valley effects on trust exists, but no clear overall conclusion was retrieved. **[preprint]** — [arXiv 2505.05543](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.05543)
- A realistic AI presenter falls under SGI labelling (IT Rules 2026) and Meta's AI-info rules, and under ASCI's virtual-influencer disclosure if it acts as a persona (sources above).

### Inferences

**Recommendation ranking (legal risk vs engagement upside):**

1. **Illustrated people + one recurring, obviously fictional narrator/mascot**
   - Legal risk: **lowest**. It is not "indistinguishable from a natural person", so SGI labelling is not triggered. There are no model-release or personality-rights issues. If the character "speaks", give it a one-line bio such as "animated narrator, voice is AI".
   - Engagement upside: moderate to good. Parasocial bonds with characters are documented, and a recurring character gives brand recognition. It fits the existing Python icon/chart pipeline.
2. **Neutral licensed stock photos of everyday Indians, captioned "representational image"**
   - Legal risk: **low if rules are kept**. Never show a stock model as a victim, debtor, loser, winner or testimonial. Never pair a face with a quote. Never imply they use or endorse the channel (Pexels "bad light" and endorsement rules).
   - Engagement upside: modest relatability.
3. **Real public figures, only for factual history/news segments**
   - Legal risk: **medium**. Personality-rights orders target commercial and AI misuse, and fair dealing for reporting/commentary is narrow. Prefer public-domain or self-drawn illustrations or text cards. Never clone a voice, animate a face, or put words or tips in their mouth.
   - Engagement upside: high for story Reels such as Harshad Mehta or the Satyam scam.
4. **AI-generated photorealistic people**
   - Legal and trust risk: **high**. Must be labelled under the IT Rules 2026 and on Meta. Disclosure lowers trust and engagement (Section 2). In a finance niche flooded with deepfake tip scams, a fake "real" person is the exact pattern audiences and regulators watch for.
   - Engagement upside: small and not unique.
5. **AI talking-head avatar presenter**
   - Risk: **highest**. It needs SGI labelling and ASCI virtual-influencer disclosure. It looks like the NSE-CEO deepfake format, and it adds subscription cost.
   - Engagement upside: the evidence is mixed-to-positive in science communication, but untested for Indian finance.

- The owner's goal of making the account "look more real" is better met by real signals: real dated data, on-screen source citations, real SEBI/RBI document screenshots, and consistent branding. Fake people do not meet it.

### Gaps
- Pixabay's current content licence wording was not retrieved (fetch blocked).
- No source showed whether Meta's "AI info" label reduces Reels reach.
- HeyGen/Synthesia pricing for 2026 was not retrieved.
- No study of Indian audiences' reactions to mascots in finance content was found.
- The status of personality-rights claims for deceased figures (e.g. Harshad Mehta) is unclear and was not researched.

## 2. Trust and engagement: AI voices vs human voices; effects of AI-disclosure labels

### Takeaway
Most studies give human voices a modest credibility edge, which is shrinking with neural TTS. AI-disclosure labels tend to reduce trust and engagement somewhat, but detailed "how we used AI" disclosures soften the drop. Disclosure is also legally required for realistic synthetic content in India from Feb 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Voice and persuasion research:
  - A COVID-myth-debunking experiment rated the human narrator more credible and more persuasive than the AI narrator. The gap was largest for narrative formats. **[peer-reviewed]** — [ResearchGate: AI vs Human Voices](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/376270955_AI_vs_Human_Voices_How_Delivery_Source_and_Narrative_Format_Influence_the_Effectiveness_of_Persuasion_Messages)
  - Older work (Stern et al. 1999 and replications) found human speech more persuasive and more credible. The gap was smaller when a computer was the stated source. **[peer-reviewed, dated]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/12542831_The_Persuasiveness_of_Synthetic_Speech_versus_Human_Speech); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/223487304_Persuasion_and_social_perception_of_human_vs_synthetic_voice_across_person_as_source_and_computer_as_source_conditions)
  - A dialect study found no overall trust difference. **[peer-reviewed]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949882125000374)
  - Perceived similarity of voices increases likability and trust. **[peer-reviewed]** — [PMC11882051](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11882051/)
- AI-disclosure research:
  - In a natural experiment plus controlled tests, AI disclosure on social posts caused persistent disengagement. More detailed disclosures explaining *how* AI was used reduced the penalty. **[journalism summarising research]** — [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ai-disclosures-social-media-disengagement)
  - Schilke & Reimann ran 13 experiments: actors who disclose AI use are trusted less, via lower perceived legitimacy. **[peer-reviewed]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389852982_The_Transparency_Dilemma_How_AI_Disclosure_Erodes_Trust)
  - An AIES experiment on AI disclosures is also relevant. **[peer-reviewed conference]** — [AIES](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/download/36671/38809/40746)
  - The Dais (Canada, n=2,472, simulated feed) found subtle labels barely change behaviour. **[think-tank]** — [Dais](https://dais.ca/reports/human-or-ai/)
  - AI labels reduce perceived accuracy but have limited broader effects. **[preprint]** — [arXiv 2506.16202](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.16202)
  - Disclosure and perceived credibility of AI-generated science content. **[peer-reviewed]** — [JCOM 2026](https://jcom.sissa.it/article/pubid/JCOM_2501_2026_A09/)

### Inferences
- An AI voice is an acceptable cost for an education account. The bigger trust lever is *what* is said (sourced, dated, no tips), not the voice itself.
- Use a specific disclosure in the bio and pinned comment, e.g. "Script researched and checked by a human; voice is AI (ElevenLabs); data from NSE/RBI, dated". This follows the SSIR finding that detailed disclosure reduces disengagement.
- If any realistic synthetic audio of a *real* person were used, it would cross into SGI and personality-rights territory. Never do this.

### Gaps
- No Hindi/Hinglish-specific study of AI vs human voice trust was found.
- The Fan, Tzirakis & Schuller 2025 paper was seen only second-hand and is not cited.

## 3. Faceless/automated account case studies and platform failure modes

### Takeaway
No reliable journalistic case study of a faceless Indian finance account was found; the search returned only vendor SEO content. The documented failure modes are platform originality rules: YouTube "inauthentic content" (15 Jul 2025) and Instagram's originality and aggregator demotion (2024, widened in 2026). Templated, same-narration slideshows are the main risk for an automated pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube (15 Jul 2025):**
  - The YPP "repetitious content" rule was renamed "inauthentic content". It targets channels with "narrative stories with only superficial differences" and "slideshows that all have the same narration". AI use is not banned if the video adds original value.
  - The penalty is demonetisation or removal from YPP. YouTube called it a clarification of an existing rule.
  - **[platform policy, via journalism]** — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/youtube-clarifies-monetisation-policy-targeting-inauthentic-content-9493155); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660); [Mediaweek](https://mediaweek.com.au/platforms-push-back-youtube-and-meta-crack-down-on-authentic-content)
- **Meta (July 2025):** accounts that repeatedly repost without meaningful changes face demotion, monetisation limits or reach removal. **[platform policy, via journalism]** — [Mediaweek](https://mediaweek.com.au/platforms-push-back-youtube-and-meta-crack-down-on-authentic-content)
- **Instagram:**
  - April 2024: aggregators that repost others' content 10+ times in 30 days are removed from recommendations, and reposts link to the original creator.
  - 2026 (Mosseri): the rule extends to photos and carousels. Accounts that mostly share content they didn't make are not recommended to non-followers. Watermarks, cropping or credit captions don't count as original.
  - **[platform policy, via journalism/vendor]** — [PetaPixel (Apr 2026)](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/); [Gigazine (2024)](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240502-instagram-updated-algorithm-prioritizes-original-content); [Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/instagram-updates-its-original-content-policy) **[vendor]**
- **Vendor claims:** finance is a high-RPM faceless niche ($8-22 RPM) and "AI voices crossed the uncanny valley". These are unverified marketing claims. **[vendor]** — [Vozo](https://www.vozo.ai/blogs/youtube/profitable-faceless-youtube-niches); [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-ai-policy-faceless-channel-future)

### Inferences
- Since this pipeline generates its own visuals, the reposting rules are not the issue. The risk is *sameness*: one voice, one layout, one narration template across hundreds of Reels.
- Per-topic original charts, varied hooks and a recurring character (option 1) all count as "added value".
- Stock or AI people pasted into templates add little originality.

### Gaps
- No credible Business Insider, Tubefilter or Indian-media case study of a growing faceless finance or history account (2023-2026) was retrieved.
- Visual-style evidence for successful accounts is therefore missing. More searching is needed if this matters.

## 4. SEBI and ASCI rules for an unregistered, education-only account (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
An unregistered account may teach concepts and use *lagged* data. It may not recommend, hint at future prices, or claim returns. From 1 July 2026 the lag is a uniform 30 days (previously 3 months for usage). Enforcement against "education" fronts is real: Avadhut Sathe, with ₹546 crore impounded in Dec 2025. Fake personas or testimonials collide with ASCI's virtual-influencer and disclosure rules and with the IT Rules' SGI labelling.

### Cited Findings
- **SEBI circular, 29 Jan 2025:**
  - People "solely engaged in education" must not use market price data of the preceding three months to "speak/talk/display the name of any security including using any code name".
  - Unregistered persons cannot give advice or make explicit or implicit performance/return claims.
  - Regulated entities cannot associate with unregistered finfluencers (building on the 22 Oct 2024 circular).
  - Penalties include monetary fines and debarment.
  - **[regulator, via journalism]** — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-finfluencer-circular-live-stock-data-market-education-rules-125013000571_1.html); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/sebi-sets-stricter-rules-on-stock-market-education-finfluencers-8674218); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/social-media/sebi-cracks-down-on-finfluencers-with-new-rules-on-stock-market-education-55026.htm); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-issues-further-clarifications-on-finfluencer-regulations); [Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-sebis-new-regulations-on-finfluencers-protecting-investors-from-unregistered-advisors)
- **2026 update:**
  - A May 2026 circular (reported) sets a uniform 30-day lag for both *sharing* (previously 1 day) and *usage* (previously 3 months) of price data for education, **effective 1 July 2026**. NISM keeps a 1-day window for its simulation lab, and audit trails are required in data-sharing agreements.
  - Educators still may not display a security's name in a way that hints at future price or direction.
  - **[regulator, via legal/journalism; circular text not read]** — [CorpLawUpdates](https://www.corplawupdates.in/updates/sebi-30-day-lag-educational-price-data-norms-2026); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelawbiz.com/securities-law/sebi/securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-revises-norms-sharing-and-usage-market-price-data-educational-purposes-533509); [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/market-news/sebi-30-day-lag-market-data-educational-content-2026/); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/sebi-revises-norms-for-sharing-and-usage-of-price-data-for-educational-purposes); [5paisa (proposal stage)](https://www.5paisa.com/news/sebi-proposes-30-day-price-data-lag-for-educational-use)
- **Avadhut Sathe enforcement:**
  - An ex parte interim order (about 4 Dec 2025) against Avadhut Sathe / ASTA found unregistered IA/RA activity under the label of "education".
  - ₹546 crore was impounded, from a ₹601 crore fee pool covering 2017-2025. Evidence came from August 2025 searches: recordings and WhatsApp stock calls in paid sessions. A March 2024 warning had been ignored.
  - SAT hearing was set for 9 Jan 2026; the outcome was not found.
  - **[regulator, via journalism]** — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelawbiz.com/corporate-law/sebi-cracks-down-on-finfluencer-avadhut-sathe-impounds-546-crore-for-providing-unregistered-investment-advice-312335); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-bars-avadhut-sathe-academy-for-unregistered-advisory-impounds-rs-546-crore); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sat-hearing-jan-9-2026-asta-appeal-sebi-order-impounding-546-crore-125121900509_1.html)
- **What counts as "general commentary":** the CFA Institute's working definition excludes macro, sector and broad-market commentary from "advice" but says this is *not* SEBI's definition. No source said a "not investment advice" disclaimer makes prohibited content lawful. **[industry research]** — [CFA Institute RPC](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/docs/surveys/rpc_clicks-and-credibility_2_online_ii.pdf)
- **ASCI:** influencer disclosure is triggered by a material connection, using labels such as Ad, Sponsored or Collaboration. Virtual influencers must disclose that they are not human. ASCI is self-regulatory, not statutory. **[self-regulatory, via law-firm]** — [Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/media-hotline/social-media-vs-reality-check-ascis-new-influencer-guidelines-4713); [Khaitan](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/ASCI-releases-influencer-advertising-guidelines-for-digital-media-Effective-14-June-21-onward)

### Inferences
- Rules for the pipeline:
  - Name individual stocks only with data that is ≥3 months old until 30 Jun 2026, and ≥30 days old from 1 Jul 2026.
  - Never frame a named security with direction words ("will rise", "target", "buy zone").
  - Broad indices and concept explainers (SIP math, expense ratios, how F&O losses happen) are lower risk, but no source confirms a formal SEBI carve-out for index commentary.
- A fake "investor" photo next to a return figure would be both an implied performance claim (SEBI) and a fake testimonial or undisclosed virtual human (ASCI). This is the single most dangerous use of people imagery here.

### Gaps
- The SEBI circular texts (Jan 2025, May 2026) were not read directly.
- No SEBI rule specifically on imagery, fake personas or AI avatars in finfluencer content was found.
- The ASCI 2023-2025 finfluencer-specific clauses (e.g. the requirement to show SEBI registration) were not retrieved in this pass.

## 5. Trust builders without monetisation

### Takeaway
No direct study was found on "no tips / no paid group" badges or on correction policies in short-form finance. The best-supported trust levers come indirectly from the regulatory and disclosure evidence above.

### Cited Findings
- Detailed, specific AI-use disclosure reduces the disengagement caused by AI labels. **[journalism on research]** — [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ai-disclosures-social-media-disengagement)
- Fraud tells that audiences are being trained to look for: NSE tells investors its staff never recommend stocks, says official information comes only via verified channels, and calls stock-tip videos with the NSE logo or CEO's face fakes. **[exchange]** — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/beware-of-deepfake-of-ceo-recommending-stocks-says-national-stock-exchange-124041000420_1.html)
- The Sathe case shows SEBI treating paid "education" with live stock calls as advice. A visible "no paid groups / no Telegram tips" stance separates an account from the enforced-against pattern. **[journalism]** — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelawbiz.com/corporate-law/sebi-cracks-down-on-finfluencer-avadhut-sathe-impounds-546-crore-for-providing-unregistered-investment-advice-312335)

### Inferences
Suggested trust-building practices:
- On-screen source and date stamps for every number ("Source: NSE, data as of 10 Sep 2026").
- A pinned bio line: "Education only. Not SEBI-registered. No tips, no paid groups, no DMs asking for money. Voice is AI; script human-checked."
- A visible corrections format ("Correction: in yesterday's Reel we said X; correct figure is Y").
- A recurring named animated narrator, clearly labelled as fictional.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found on source citations, correction notices, or "no tips" pledges and follower trust in Indian finfluencer content. Validate these through A/B tests on the account itself.
